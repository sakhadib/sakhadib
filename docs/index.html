About

I study what large language models can and cannot do in the places mainstream benchmarks skip: low-resource languages such as Bangla, figurative and legal text, and the political and safety behaviour of models once the language or the framing changes. I try to build datasets and evaluation protocols that could falsify a claim about model capability, not just confirm it.

My first-author paper on ideological plasticity in LLMs received the Best Paper Award at AACL-IJCNLP 2026. It argues that a model's political position is better described as a shape over contexts than as a single point. I graduated from IUT in October 2026 with First Class with Honours (CGPA 3.84/4.00), where I did my research in the Systems and Software Lab.

Research

01

Multilingual & low-resource NLP

Resources and benchmarks for Bangla: a 10,361-idiom corpus, a corpus of Bangladeshi legal acts from 1799 to 2025, and studies of why models read idioms literally and retrieve unevenly across languages.

02

LLM evaluation

Evaluation designed to test assumptions rather than confirm them: what benchmarks actually measure, consistency in temporal reasoning, and game-based protocols for multi-turn behaviour.

03

Safety in low-resource languages

The gap between a model's ability to understand harmful or sensitive content and its ability to contain it, in Bangla derogatory speech and in adversarial multi-turn dialogue.

04

LLMs & social science

Measuring the political behaviour of language models with instruments from political science and psychometrics, treating ideology as a context-conditioned distribution.

Datasets

  • Bangla Bagdhara — 10,361 Bengali idioms with a 19-field semantic, syntactic and cultural annotation schema, plus the benchmark, model outputs and human-evaluation protocol from the LREC 2026 paper.
  • Bangladesh Legal Acts Dataset — 1,484 acts from 1799 to 2025, split into 35,633 sections, in English, Bangla and mixed text, each annotated with the legal system in force at the time.
